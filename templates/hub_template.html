Test Strip Lot Checking Program

Drug test strips help prevent drug overdoses. People can use these strips anywhere, and studies show the results actually change behavior — people report not using, using less, or using in ways that are less likely to cause overdose, like with someone nearby (Goldman et al., 2019). Because they are such a valuable tool, it is critical that they work right.

Most of the time, the strips worked. In plain water, fentanyl strips were right __STORY_FTS_WATER__% of the time. When fentanyl was there, they found it about __STORY_FTS_IN10__ in 10 times.

Xylazine strips gave a clear answer in about __STORY_XTS_IN10__ of 10 tests. About __STORY_XTS_FAINT__% of tests gave a faint line that was too hard to call.

Two limits worth knowing about

  • Faint test lines. At low amounts, some strips show a very faint test lineExample of a very faint test line (see the arrow).. It is easy to miss, especially in poor light. For fentanyl strips we count any visible line as a negative result. For xylazine strips we call a faint line “inconclusive.”

  • False alarms from other drugs. Sometimes a strip says it found the drug when it did not. For fentanyl strips, __STORY_FTS_TOP_INTERFERENT__ is the most common reason (__STORY_FTS_TOP_LOTS__ of __FTS_LOTS__ batches). For xylazine strips, similar medicines like __STORY_XTS_TOP_INTERFERENTS__ can do it.

Even so, __STORY_FTS_PERFECT__ of the __FTS_LOTS__ fentanyl batches found fentanyl every time and never gave a false alarm in plain water.
