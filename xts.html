Detection of xylazine

If your tap water is “hard” it contains a lot of calcium and magnesium ions, which can interfere with lateral-flow strip chemistry. You may get better results by using DI (deionized) water.
Detected (no test line) Inconclusive (faint test line, 1–3 out of 10) Missed (clear test line, 4–10 out of 10)
Method. All figures on this page are based on reports to the password-protected lot checking data collector form. If your lab wants to participate, contact Prof. Lieberman. A lot is identified by Brand + Lot number; expiration dates are recorded but not used for matching, since testers occasionally mistype them. Three true-positive panels are tracked separately for 2500 ng/mL xylazine in DI water, 2500 ng/mL in (hard) tap water, and 1000 ng/mL in DI water — comparing the first two isolates the effect of water hardness; comparing DI at two concentrations shows sensitivity near the low end. True negative results are from testing with DI water (n = 58 pooled). Result reading. Testers score each test line's visibility on a 1–10 intensity scale (10 = dark and obvious, 1 = so faint viewers disagree whether it's there), or record a flat “Positive” when the control line only was seen. Each detection read is sorted into three groups: detected (a flat “Positive”, no test line), inconclusive (a test line of intensity 1–3, so faint that a reader in imperfect lighting is likely to miss it, and not safe to call either way) and missed (a clear test line, intensity 4–10). The bars show all three, and the headline percentage is the detected share only. For the interference matrix, a faint line (1–3) is still counted as a hit, the conservative choice there. Cells with a range (e.g. “5–10”) or a comma-separated list use the lowest value in that cell, the most conservative reading. Thumbnails use the first product/packaging or strip photo uploaded per lot (product/packaging and strip photos are both pulled in and merged into one gallery); a hash of each photo's contents is used to skip duplicate uploads of the same image. Refresh is manual for now. Chemical structure thumbnails on the interference matrix page are skeletal formulas from Wikimedia Commons (public domain; click a structure to open its source Wikipedia article).
