Detection of xylazine

DI (deionized) water has very low ion content; our tap water is high in calcium and magnesium ions, which can interfere with lateral-flow strip chemistry. Comparing DI vs. tap at the same concentration isolates that effect.
Method. All figures on this page are based on reports to the password-protected lot checking data collector form. If your lab wants to participate, contact Prof. Lieberman. A lot is identified by Brand + Lot number; expiration dates are recorded but not used for matching, since testers occasionally mistype them. Three true-positive panels are tracked separately for 2500 ng/mL xylazine in DI water, 2500 ng/mL in (hard) tap water, and 1000 ng/mL in DI water — comparing the first two isolates the effect of water hardness; comparing DI at two concentrations shows sensitivity near the low end. True negative results are from testing with DI water (n = 58 pooled). Result reading. Testers score each test line's visibility on a 1–10 intensity scale (10 = dark and obvious, 1 = so faint viewers disagree whether it's there), or record a flat “Positive” when the control line only was seen. Per the lab's guidance, intensities of 1–3 are treated as effectively unreadable and counted the same as “Positive” for both detection and interference purposes, since a real-world reader in imperfect lighting is likely to miss a line that faint. Cells with a range (e.g. “5–10”) or a comma-separated list use the lowest value in that cell, the most conservative reading. Thumbnails use the first product/packaging photo uploaded per lot; refresh is manual for now.
